# IsoformVUS: Exon Architecture, Alternative Isoform Expression, and NMD in Rare-Disease Variant Interpretation

**Author:** Sai Krishna Uppula
**Repository:** [github.com/Saikrishnnauppula/IsoformVUS](https://github.com/Saikrishnnauppula/IsoformVUS)
**Genome Build & Annotations:** GRCh38, GENCODE v46 for the exon matrix (v44 only for the E8 annotation-fragility experiment), [MANE Select v1.5](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04558-8), GTEx v8 (quantified on GENCODE v26; see Methods)

## Abstract

Clinical genome sequencing returns many variants of uncertain significance (VUS). Standard predictors such as CADD (Kircher et al. 2014), [SpliceAI](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(18)31629-5) (Jaganathan et al. 2019), and [AlphaMissense](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adg7492) (Cheng et al. 2023) score coding variants on the canonical transcript, with little use of exon architecture or alternative isoform expression. We analyzed 587,227 ClinVar variants and a gene-held-out benchmark of 60,521 labeled variants (30,146 pathogenic or likely pathogenic; 30,375 benign or likely benign) across 102 Mendelian disease genes.

Auditing the tiered models M1-M5 took three runs. In the first, a 6,077-variant tie block penalized the discrete M1 baseline by -0.0318 in step average precision, so the apparent M2/M3 gain of +0.031 was a tie artifact. In the second, rebuilding the matrix with real CADD values let test-time CADD signal enter the tiers through a coefficient fit on a constant — CADD was scored for validation and test variants only, never for train — and inflated every tier to about 0.94; that run's "M2 is the best tier" result is withdrawn. In the third run (2026-11-09), CADD was removed from every fitted tier and kept as an external baseline only. On that refit, isoform structural flags (M4) raise tie-corrected AUPRC from 0.8336 (M1) to 0.8526 (+0.0189; gene-clustered 95% CI [+0.0107, +0.0278] over the 24 test genes), while phenotype (M2, +0.0007) and cell-type (M3, +0.0014) features add nothing — their gene-clustered intervals include zero — and tissue isoform usage (M5) adds nothing over M4 (-0.0000). The first run's step-AUPRC gain of +0.0509 overstates the M4 effect, because step evaluation penalizes the tied M1 baseline. Resolved missense pathogenicity in internal coding exons fell from 73.00% (3,741 of 5,125) in compact constitutive symmetric exons to 1.17% (3 of 257) in giant backup-skipped symmetric exons, an odds ratio of 0.0044. That last cell is one gene, TTN (4 exons, 3 pathogenic variants, 94.0% of its missense unresolved), and the gradient is a description of ClinVar, not a causal claim about exon length. Premature termination codons were 99.93% pathogenic across all exon states, which is what the PVS1 decision tree predicts, not a test of nonsense-mediated decay. A further model (M6) reached pooled test AUROC 0.9923, but a consequence-class-only model reached 0.9848, and within missense M6 (0.9321) did not beat the M1 baseline (0.9511), so the pooled gain is consequence-class separation. We withdrew the cell-level Strong Benign assignments and the VUS relabelling, the donor-edge finding, the antisense-oligonucleotide candidate framing, the three isoform-regime rates, and the AlphaMissense mediation percentages; the exon list is a computational nomination with no functional evidence. The test set was accessed thirteen times as recorded in results/test_access.log (seventeen reads in all, counting four unlogged script-18 evaluations), thresholds were set from analyses over all 102 genes, and no RNA-level validation was performed.

## 1. Introduction

Exome and genome sequencing for suspected Mendelian disorders returns rare variants whose clinical significance is often uncertain. CADD, SpliceAI, and AlphaMissense give sequence- and structure-based scores, yet most pipelines score coding consequences on a single canonical transcript, usually MANE Select, without using the exon architecture of the gene or the expression of alternative isoforms across human tissues ([Cummings et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7334198/)).

In the first stages of IsoformVUS (Stages 01-17), we assembled 60,521 ClinVar-labeled variants across 102 Mendelian disease genes and trained five sequential logistic-regression models (M1: variant scores; M2: +HPO phenotype; M3: +cell-type expression; M4: +isoform structural flags; M5: +tissue isoform usage). On the first run, M4 improved standard step AUPRC from 0.8016 to 0.8524 (+0.0509, 95% CI [0.0473, 0.0546]). Two audits changed that reading. The step gain overstates the effect: a 6,077-variant tie block penalizes M1 under step evaluation, and the tie-corrected gain is +0.0189. And the rebuilt matrix that appeared to kill the gain (M1 step AUPRC 0.9465, M4 0.9395) was itself an artifact: real CADD values in validation and test entered the tiers through a coefficient fit on a constant in train, because no train variant was ever scored for CADD. The 2026-11-09 refit, with CADD removed from every tier, is the comparison this paper reports (M1 tie-corrected AUPRC 0.8336, M4 0.8526). Diagnostic audits then raised two questions: why did shuffling the HPO and GTEx tables in M2 and M3 still produce an apparent +0.031 AUPRC gain over M1, and why did continuous isoform-usage weighting in M5 add nothing over binary structural flags in M4?

A journal review panel rejected the earlier version of this paper. This version is rewritten around the panel's findings: the tie artifact and the consequence-class confound are the central results, the exon gradient is a descriptive observation, and the clinical and therapeutic claims are withdrawn. The three isoform regimes from the earlier version are withdrawn in full (Section 6.1, item 8).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Data and splits

We downloaded the ClinVar weekly release of 2026-10-04 and retained 587,227 variants in 102 Mendelian disease genes. We built the benchmark from 60,521 labeled variants (30,146 pathogenic or likely pathogenic; 30,375 benign or likely benign) and split it by gene: 61 train genes (35,921 variants), 17 validation genes (10,200 variants), and 24 test genes (14,400 variants), with zero gene overlap. We assigned splits with a deterministic SHA-256 shuffle at seed 20261006, stratified by disease category. Figure 02 summarises the workflow from ClinVar download to the locked test set. We reconstructed 4,593 MANE Select exons, of which 4,509 were internal coding exons. We used GENCODE v46 for the exon matrix and MANE Select v1.5 (Morales et al. 2022) throughout. GENCODE v44 appears only in the E8 annotation-fragility experiment, which compared v44 and v46 consequence calls.

We took isoform usage from GTEx v8 median transcript TPM. GTEx v8 was quantified on GENCODE v26, and we joined those transcript IDs to our GENCODE v46 structural classification on the ENST base (the accession without its version number). The unmatched-transcript counts were written to data/interim/, which is not in the shipped repository, so the mapping loss is unquantified here. Figure 03 shows the per-variant effect classification on MANE Select versus all transcripts and the GTEx isoform-usage evidence behind it (7,057 gene-by-transcript-by-tissue rows). Backup skipping, as we use the term, is a transcript-abundance fraction: an alternative protein-coding transcript with at least 10% usage and at least 1.0 median TPM skips the exon. It is not a measurement of splice-junction usage. We computed no junction PSI.

We scored CADD v1.7 through its REST API for 24,600 validation and test variants. No train variant was scored, so every train row carries cadd_phred = -1.0 with cadd_missing = 1 (19,014 of 60,521 matrix rows scored). The 2026-11-08 rebuild put those scores into the fitted tiers, and the benchmark was re-run from it; because the fitted CADD coefficient was never fit against varying CADD values, test-time CADD signal entered every tier through an uncalibrated coefficient and inflated them (M1 step AUPRC 0.9465). On 2026-11-09 we removed cadd_phred and cadd_missing from every fitted tier and re-ran the benchmark (scripts/10_11_benchmark.py, scripts/14_add_baselines.py, scripts/19_refit_statistics.py); the refit values are the ones reported in Section 3.1, and CADD appears only as an external baseline (Section 5.1). Querying CADD for the 35,921 train variants would let a future version fit CADD as a feature; we did not do it here. We used AlphaMissense v1 scores as released. We scored SpliceAI v1.3.1 locally; its bundled annotation dates to the GENCODE V24 era, while the rest of the analysis uses v46. We took HPO release 2026-02-16. The shipped matrix holds no gnomAD allele-frequency column and no REVEL score, although a script to fetch gnomAD v4 frequencies exists in the repository and was never run to completion.

### 2.2 Models

We fit M1-M5 as logistic-regression tiers on train genes only. Figure 01 shows the five tiers and their feature counts on the 2026-11-09 refit (M1: 7 features; M2: 9; M3: 12; M4: 21; M5: 24). No fitted tier uses CADD: cadd_phred and cadd_missing were removed from every tier on 2026-11-09, because CADD was scored for validation and test variants only and both columns are constant in train (Section 2.1). CADD appears in this paper only as an external baseline. M6 added exon-architecture, NMD-zone, and splice-boundary features to the M1 logit, including consequence-class indicators (is_ptv, is_syn, mane_sev, and related terms) and interaction terms between consequence class and architecture. For internal missense variants, M6 fits exon-architecture offsets on train genes only, with the M1 logit held fixed (scripts/18_exon_architecture_nmd.py). The fitted donor-edge coefficient is +0.31 (95% CI [-0.86, 1.48]) on 38 train donor-edge variants. The SpliceAI interaction terms are not identified: no train missense variant carries a SpliceAI score, so their coefficients are 0. The earlier +3.60 and +1.80 constants were hard-coded, not fitted, and are gone.

### 2.3 Exon matrix and cutoffs

We classified each internal exon by nucleotide length (compact at most 180 bp, medium 181-400 bp, large 401-1,000 bp, giant over 1,000 bp), by backup skipping (Skip = 1 when an alternative protein-coding transcript with at least 10% usage and at least 1.0 median TPM skips the exon), and by phase symmetry (exon length divisible by 3, so skipping preserves the downstream reading frame). We set every cutoff from analyses over all 102 genes, test genes included, and none was pre-registered. We applied no multiplicity correction to the exon-matrix analyses.

### 2.4 Test-set access and statistics

We evaluated models on the 24 test genes (n = 14,400). We accessed the test set thirteen times as recorded in results/test_access.log: one benchmark run on 2026-10-06, eight baseline-enrichment evaluations on 2026-10-07, two benchmark reruns and one baseline run on 2026-11-08, and one script-18 M6 evaluation on 2026-11-08. Four earlier script-18 M6 evaluations on 2026-11-08 were not logged by that script, so the test split was read seventeen times in all during this revision. The effective sample size for the exon-matrix analyses is genes and exons, not variants: 102 genes and 4,509 internal exons carry 311,249 coding variants, and variants in one exon or gene are not independent observations. Variant-level confidence intervals treat variants as independent and understate uncertainty; for the tier differences in Section 3.1 we also report gene-clustered intervals (cluster bootstrap over the 24 test genes, scripts/19_refit_statistics.py), and those are the intervals the tier claims rest on. Of the 60,521 benchmark labels, 40,404 (66.8%) are single-submitter (1-star) classifications.

## 3. The Tie Artifact and the M1-M5 Tiers

### 3.1 Resolution of the M2/M3 tie artifact

In the 14,400-variant test split, 6,077 single-nucleotide variants, mostly intronic and synonymous, lack AlphaMissense scores and carry identical values on every M1 feature, so they received an identical predicted probability under M1. Standard right-endpoint step evaluation of average precision (`sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score`) scores a tied block at its worst-case rank, which penalized M1 by -0.0318 against the exact expectation under uniform random tie-breaking ([McSherry & Najork 2008](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/computing-information-retrieval-performance-measures-efficiently-in-the-presence-of-tied-scores/)). M2 and M3 add gene-level constants, which break the inter-gene tie block without changing within-gene variant ordering. The table below is the 2026-11-09 refit: step values from results/benchmark.json, tie-corrected values from results/statistics.json (tie_corrected_refit_no_cadd). Figure 04 plots the same tier comparison. The earlier version of this table mixed a locked run with a refit run, and the values that matched no shipped file are gone.

| Model Tier | Test AUROC | Standard Step AUPRC | Tie-Corrected AUPRC (McSherry & Najork 2008) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| M1 (AlphaMissense + review stars + variant type) | 0.7985 | 0.8015 | 0.8336 |
| M2 (+HPO Phenotype Counts) | 0.7969 | 0.8328 | 0.8343 (+0.0007) |
| M3 (+Cell-Type Relevance) | 0.7975 | 0.8335 | 0.8350 (+0.0014) |
| M4 (+Isoform Structural Flags) | 0.8029 | 0.8524 | 0.8526 (+0.0189) |
| M5 (+Dominant Isoform Usage) | 0.8028 | 0.8523 | 0.8526 (+0.0189) |

Tie-corrected values are from results/statistics.json (expected average precision under uniform tie-breaking). Step and tie-corrected AUPRC differ most for M1 (0.8015 against 0.8336): the tie block sits in M1's scores, and the step values for M2 and M3 (0.8328, 0.8335) borrow most of their apparent gain from breaking it.

The tie artifact explains the M2/M3 step gains, not the M4 gain. On the refit, M2 and M3 add +0.0007 and +0.0014 in tie-corrected AUPRC over M1, and the gene-clustered 95% CI for M2-M1 over the 24 test genes is [-0.0029, +0.0045]: neither layer shows a gain. The rebuilt run's "M2 is the best tier at 0.9533" is withdrawn (Section 6.1, item 15): on that run the tiers carried CADD signal that had entered through a constant-fit coefficient. M4 adds +0.0189 in tie-corrected AUPRC over M1 (variant-level 95% CI [+0.0154, +0.0228]; gene-clustered 95% CI [+0.0107, +0.0278]), so the isoform structural flags carry a real gain, at a third of the size the first run's step values suggested. In AUROC the M4-M1 difference is +0.0043 (95% CI [-0.0001, +0.0086]), which does not reach significance. M5 adds nothing over M4 (tie-corrected -0.0000; AUROC -0.0001). Figure 05 plots these paired AUROC differences. Top-k recovery tells the same story in counts: M5's top 50 test variants include 23 pathogenic variants that M1's top 50 missed (Figure 06; results/benchmark.json). The earlier claim that 94.5% of M4's gain over M3 reflected canonical coding-sequence membership is deleted: no combination of the shipped numbers gives 94.5%. M4's flags record whether a variant overlaps a protein-coding transcript, and 99 of the 102 genes carry non-coding or partial GENCODE transcripts that omit canonical exons, so the flags largely restate coding-sequence membership. That is a reading of the code, not a quantified share: the one-bit indicator comparison behind the 94.5% figure matches no shipped results file.

The three isoform regimes reported in the earlier version of this section are withdrawn in full (Section 6.1, item 8). The regime table and the code that built it are not in the shipped repository: the 587k variant table that carried the regime classification sits in data/interim/script18/, which is not shipped, and the builder code was deleted with the experiment archive. None of the three rates can be regenerated from shipped materials, so none of them is reported here.

Pooled across length and skipping, resolved missense pathogenicity falls 18.1-fold from compact constitutive exons (72.26%) to giant backup-skipped exons (3.98%), and 62.4-fold from cell 1B (73.00%) to cell 4D (1.17%), an odds ratio of 0.0044. We report no p-values for these odds ratios: the CMH p-values treat variants as independent within genes (the shipped values were 4.29 x 10^-187 and 3.53 x 10^-42), the effective sample size is genes and exons, not the 16,050 resolved missense variants in 4,509 exons in 102 genes, and we fit no gene- or exon-clustered model. The odds ratios are descriptive values only. Script 18 now withholds the p-values at source. The composition of the decisive cell makes the point directly: cell 4D holds 4 exons in a single gene, TTN, with 3 pathogenic and 254 benign resolved missense variants. Of the 4,298 missense variants in 4D, 4,041 (94.0%) are unresolved, so the 1.17% rate describes the 257 variants curators could resolve, not the cell. Across the whole 587k table, 203,576 variants (34.7%) are VUS and 52,918 (9.0%) are conflicting, so 43.7% are unresolved, and resolvability itself tracks evidence strength.

Frameshift and nonsense variants show no such gradient: resolved PTVs are 99.77-100.00% pathogenic in every cell, 78,284 pathogenic against 53 benign overall (99.93%). Nine of the 16 cells contain zero benign frameshifts and eight contain zero benign stops, so the outcome has almost no variance left for any exon feature to explain. That pattern is what the PVS1 decision tree predicts for a panel enriched for haploinsufficient loss-of-function genes (Abou Tayoun et al. 2018): a predicted loss-of-function variant in such a gene is classified pathogenic or likely pathogenic by rule. It does not test nonsense-mediated decay, and this paper does not present it as a test. The earlier reading, that PTVs stay pathogenic because NMD fires regardless of dispensability, is withdrawn.

### 4.2 Scores that do not use ClinVar labels, and the AlphaMissense overlap

AlphaMissense was trained on human and primate missense variation and benchmarked on ClinVar, and CADD was trained on fixed human-chimpanzee differences against simulated variants. Neither was fit to ClinVar pathogenic/benign labels, but both are the kind of evidence curators use for PP3/BP4, so they are not independent of the curation process. We call them scores that do not use ClinVar labels, not label-independent controls.

Median AlphaMissense falls from 0.943 in compact constitutive exons to 0.104 in giant backup-skipped exons, while median CADD PHRED moves only from 26.60 to 20.90. We dropped the per-kilobase density columns from the earlier version: ClinVar counts have no testing-volume denominator, so variants per kilobase are submission rates as much as biology. Six genes (TTN, BRCA2, ATM, NF1, APC, BRCA1) contribute about 22% of all variants in the table.

In the earlier version we reported counterfactual mediation: 77.9% of the large/giant exon effect and 94.7% of the backup-skipping effect mediated by AlphaMissense. Those numbers are withdrawn (Section 6.1, item 10). The generating code is not in the repository, the values were carried over verbatim into results/exon_architecture_nmd.json, and they cannot be regenerated from shipped materials. AlphaMissense is a trained predictor, not a measured biological mediator, and it is a function of the same sequence features that define the exon states, so the assumptions a causal mediation reading needs cannot hold for it. The exon gradient tracks structural constraint. Whether exon length adds anything beyond that constraint is untested, because we fit no model that adjusts length for disorder, domain content, or within-gene position.

Two published NMD rules bear directly on the giant exons and were missing from the earlier version. Lindeboom et al. (2016, Nature Genetics 48:1112-1118) showed that premature termination codons within 150 nucleotides of the start codon often fail to trigger NMD, through translation re-initiation, and that PTCs in exons longer than about 400 nucleotides escape NMD more often, with NMD efficiency near 61% against 98% in shorter exons. Our flagship cells are giant exons over 1,000 bp, exactly where NMD is predicted weakest, and we modelled neither rule. A PTV in a giant exon may therefore produce a truncated protein rather than undergo decay, and its near-uniform pathogenic classification would then reflect curation practice rather than NMD. EJC-dependent NMD is translation-dependent: the exon-junction complex is deposited during splicing, and decay is triggered by the pioneer round of translation after export to the cytoplasm. Nothing in this study shows decay occurring co-transcriptionally, and no RNA data of any kind were collected. The title was changed accordingly.

Among resolved internal PTVs, 53 are benign or likely benign out of 78,337 (0.068%). Of those 53, 21 (39.62%) lie within 5 bp of an exon-intron junction, against 9.56% of pathogenic internal PTVs (odds ratio 6.21). The count is small, and junction proximity was not tested as a mechanism, so we report it as an observation: benign PTVs cluster near junctions, and splice rescue is one untested explanation among others.

### 4.3 In-frame indels and NMD zones

In-frame deletions were more often pathogenic than in-frame insertions in compact and medium constitutive exons (97.7%, 1,155 of 1,182 resolved, against 82.4%, 566 of 687; odds ratio 9.15). In large and giant backup-skipped exons, resolved pathogenicity fell to 24.2% for deletions (22 of 91) and 35.2% for insertions (43 of 122).

For premature termination codons (n = 87,779 across the 102 genes), distance to the 3'-most exon-exon junction ([Nagy & Maquat 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9644970/)) gives four regimes:

| PTC zone | n | Raw P/LP % | VUS/conflicting % | Resolved P/LP % |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Canonical internal NMD-trigger zone (over 50 nt upstream of the final exon-exon junction) | 80,127 | 96.66% | 1.87% | 99.93% (77,452 : 51) |
| Penultimate-exon 50-nt EJC escape zone (1-50 nt upstream of the final exon-exon junction) | 928 | 89.66% | 8.73% (81/928) | not reported |
| Last-exon proximal truncation (in last exon, under 90% of CDS) | 4,298 | 94.00% | 4.77% | 99.90% (4,040 : 4) |
| Last-exon distal C-terminal tail (in last exon, 90% or more of CDS) | 2,426 | 70.20% | 27.37% (664/2,426) | 97.71% (1,703 : 40) |

These zones use only the 50-nt and last-exon rules. They do not separate PTCs that escape NMD under the start-proximal or long-exon rules (Section 4.2), so each zone mixes PTCs with different predicted NMD efficiency. In C-terminal degron genes such as ASXL3, NMD-escaping truncations can act through gain of function (Nakamura et al. 2026, medRxiv preprint).

## 5. Model Evaluation and Computationally Nominated Exons

### 5.1 M6 evaluated against a consequence-aware baseline

M6 added exon-architecture, NMD-zone, and donor-edge features to the M1 logit and reached pooled test AUROC 0.9923 (tie-corrected AUPRC 0.9928, Brier 0.0320) across the 24 test genes. That number is dominated by consequence class. A consequence-class-only logistic regression, fit on train genes and evaluated on the same test genes, reached AUROC 0.9848 (tie-corrected AUPRC 0.9856, Brier 0.0447). For scale, CADD alone — a single score with no model on top — scores AUROC 0.9554 and AUPRC 0.9590 on the 11,240 test variants that carry a CADD value, which beats M5 (AUPRC 0.8523 on the full test set, 2026-11-09 refit) and every tier M1–M5 on AUPRC (results/benchmark.json). The denominators differ: the tiers are scored on all 14,400 test variants, CADD only on the 11,240 scored. The test set is 9.5% missense (1,368 of 14,400) and is dominated by classes whose labels are near-deterministic: frameshift 99.97% pathogenic, stop-gained 99.94%, synonymous 0.20%, intronic 3.47%, missense 68.64%. Within missense, the only class where exon architecture could add signal, M6 scored AUROC 0.9321 against M4 at 0.9493 and M1 at 0.9511. M6 was worse than the M1 baseline within missense. The pooled comparison in the earlier version, M1 against M6 at 0.9930, compared a model with consequence-class features against models without them, and it is withdrawn as a lead claim. Script 18 refits M1 with a standardized logistic regression; its pooled M1 (AUROC 0.7981) is a different estimator from the benchmark's M1 (AUROC 0.7985 on the 2026-11-09 refit), and the within-missense comparison is internal to script 18. M6's donor-edge offsets are now fit on train genes only (Section 2.2): the donor-edge coefficient is +0.31 (95% CI [-0.86, 1.48]), and the fitted model recalls 0 of 7 held-out edge variants, against 2 of 7 without the offsets. The donor-edge recall claim is withdrawn (Section 6.1).

We checked the gradient and the benchmark against seven sensitivity checks.

1. Leave-top-genes-out jackknife. Excluding BRCA1, BRCA2, APC, TTN, and NF1, the internal missense gradient held at 73.39% in compact constitutive exons against 7.05% in giant backup-skipped exons (CMH odds ratio 0.2348 for size over 400 bp; 0.5043 for backup skipping). The jackknife cannot test cell 4D: excluding TTN removes the cell entirely.
2. Review-status filtering (at least 2 stars). Restricting to at least 2-star ClinVar variants gave 85.48% pathogenic missense in compact constitutive exons (n = 1,315) against 5.38% in giant backup-skipped exons (n = 93), while PTVs in giant backup-skipped exons stayed 100.00% pathogenic (n = 272). The 587k table carries no review-star column, so this check rests on the exon JSON alone.
3. Mutational spectrum control. C>T and G>A transitions fell 18.7-fold (67.94% to 3.64%), matching the 17.7-fold fall across all other substitutions (74.47% to 4.20%).
4. AlphaMissense strata within exon size. At AlphaMissense over 0.70, resolved missense pathogenicity was 97.44% (n = 2,577) in compact exons against 90.05% (n = 201) in large or giant exons; in the middle stratum (0.30-0.70) it was 81.29% (n = 481) against 48.48% (n = 99); at AlphaMissense under 0.30 it was 22.18% (n = 807) against 6.16% (n = 552). The exon-size effect persists within AlphaMissense strata.
5. Phase symmetry and PTV pathogenicity. Resolved PTV pathogenicity in phase-symmetric backup-skipped internal exons was 99.986% (7,330 of 7,331) against 99.966% (14,750 of 14,755) in asymmetric exons. That is consistent with PVS1; it is not evidence about splicing.
6. Split integrity. Train (61 genes), validation (17 genes), and test (24 genes) splits share zero genes and zero exons.
7. Temporal holdout (post-2024 classifications). Among 2,408 internal missense variants newly resolved between 2024 and 2026, pathogenicity fell from 78.61% (n = 1,183) in compact constitutive exons to 6.45% (n = 93) in giant backup-skipped exons. The source file sits in data/interim/script18/, not the shipped data tree, and reclassifications use the same ACMG evidence types as the original labels, so the holdout is not independent ground truth.

### 5.3 One worked counterfactual and two literature case studies

A single LMNA variant (chr1:156115000:C>T) shows what isoform context does to one score. Across 45 GENCODE v46 LMNA transcripts the variant is coding-specific on some transcripts and intronic or silent on others. Under M5 it scores 0.9467 if the isoform context is assumed irrelevant and 0.9883 with the observed isoform context (Δ +0.0417; results/counterfactual.json; Figures 07 and 08). The variant ranks 8th by M5 among isoform-specific recovered variants (results/recovered_variants.tsv). This is one variant under M5, not a benchmark result. M5's tie-corrected gain over M1 (+0.0189) is M4's gain: the usage features add nothing over the structural flags (-0.0000, Section 3.1), and the counterfactual is built on M5.

Two published mechanisms motivate the approach but are not results of this paper. Ly et al. (2025) showed isoform-selective TRNT1 alleles in patients with sideroblastic anemia, immunodeficiency, fevers, and developmental delay (SIFD), including a start-codon variant (M30L) that selectively disrupts one TRNT1 isoform. TRNT1 has 13 protein-coding transcripts with distinct exon structures in GENCODE v46 (results/case_study_candidates.md). Cheng et al. (2026) used the AlphaGenome Variant Impact (AVI) score to rank an epileptic-encephalopathy VUS in DNM1 intron 10 top among the proband's de novo variants; the predicted cryptic splice acceptor creates a 13-amino-acid in-frame extension of the brain-specific exon 10a, which is absent from MANE Select, and saturation mutagenesis supported the predicted splice effect. Neither gene was analysed with IsoformVUS models in this paper. They are literature case studies, not validation.

### 6.2 Limitations

Our panel covers 102 Mendelian disease genes enriched for haploinsufficient and loss-of-function mechanisms, and the gradient may not extend to other gene sets. Two-thirds of the benchmark labels are single-submitter classifications, which are the most likely to rest on PVS1 alone. Only resolved variants enter the gradient, and 43.7% of the 587k table is unresolved, with 94.0% unresolved in cell 4D. The resolved subset is a selected sample, and we ran no analysis that includes VUS, such as a three-outcome model or a bounding analysis.

No cutoff in this paper was pre-registered, and we applied no multiplicity correction to the exon-matrix analyses. For the exon-matrix analyses we fit no gene- or exon-clustered model, so their confidence intervals treat variants as independent and understate uncertainty; the tier differences in Section 3.1 are the exception, and carry gene-clustered intervals. We fit no model that adjusts exon length for disorder, domain content, or within-gene position, so the gradient is a description, not an estimate of a length effect.

The GTEx v8 transcript IDs were joined to GENCODE v46 on the ENST base, and the mapping loss is unquantified because the provenance counts were not shipped. Backup skipping is a transcript-abundance fraction, not a junction measurement, and we computed no junction PSI. No RNA-level validation exists for any skipping, NMD, or exon-nomination claim in this paper: no carrier RNA-seq, no minigene or splicing reporter, no patient RNA, no allele-specific expression, and no NMD-inhibition experiment.

The test set was accessed thirteen times as recorded in results/test_access.log, and four earlier script-18 evaluations were not logged, so the test split was read seventeen times in all during this revision. The log itself sat untracked in the working tree until 2026-11-09, because .gitignore carried a bare *.log rule; a negation (!results/test_access.log) now keeps it tracked. Thresholds and constants were set from analyses over all 102 genes including test genes, so the "held-out" label applies only to the final logistic fit. The M6 donor-edge offsets are now fit on train genes only (Section 2.2), but the SpliceAI interaction terms are not identified, because no train missense variant carries a SpliceAI score. The feature matrix carries real CADD values for validation and test variants only (19,014 of 60,521 scored; 0 of 35,921 train rows). The 2026-11-08 rebuilt benchmark fit its CADD coefficient on that constant and let test-time CADD signal inflate every tier; its values (M1 0.9465, M4 0.9395, M2 0.9533) are superseded and marked as such in results/statistics.json. The 2026-11-09 refit removes CADD from all fitted tiers (scripts/19_refit_statistics.py recomputes results/statistics.json and results/per_model_cis.json on the refit); CADD remains an external baseline, and on its scored subset it beats every tier on AUPRC (Section 5.1). The pre-rebuild M2-M1 (+0.0310) and M3-M1 (+0.0317) step-AUPRC differences are tie artifacts; on the refit they are +0.0007 and +0.0014 tie-corrected, with gene-clustered intervals including zero. The results summary records 4,593 reconstructed MANE exons and 4,509 internal coding exons. The earlier 4,132/3,955 matches no reconstruction run. The counts move with sort tie-ordering (a stable sort gives 4,578/4,494), so the reconstruction has no tie-break rule for same-position variants. The 16-cell tensor in results/exon_architecture_nmd.json summed 4,528 exons against the 4,509 internal count: 19 exons carry mixed backup-skip values across their variants and were counted in two cells when exons were counted on the variant-level frame. Per-cell exon counts are now taken from the exon-level frame (backup skipping resolved per exon as the maximum over its variants) and sum to 4,509; the variant-level counts of those 19 exons remain split between cells, because reassigning them needs the script-18 inputs. Script 18 now resolves exon attributes per exon before cell assignment. The repository's run script (scripts/run_all.sh) now lists 19 stages, each calling a real script; the six stub stages were deleted or implemented. The five large input files script 18 needs sit in data/interim/script18/, not the shipped tree, and the code that built them was removed with the experiment archive, so they cannot currently be regenerated from the repository. Figures 09–12 (exon architecture and NMD, structural mediation controls, donor–acceptor boundary asymmetry, exon-skipping candidates) are archival images from the stage-18 run: no script in the repository generates them, and no claim in this paper rests on them. Figure 11 shows the donor–acceptor boundary asymmetry behind the withdrawn donor-edge finding (Section 6.1); it is retained only as a record of what was withdrawn, not as evidence. The ClinVar weekly release the locked results were built from (clinvar_20261004.vcf.gz) now returns HTTP 404 from NCBI FTP; stage 01 pins the live weekly clinvar_20261108.vcf.gz (verified 2026-11-09) with a fallback to the unversioned file, so a fresh download no longer reproduces the locked inputs bit-for-bit. SpliceAI features were scored against a 2018-era annotation while the rest of the analysis uses v46. No gnomAD allele-frequency feature and no REVEL score exist in the shipped matrix.
